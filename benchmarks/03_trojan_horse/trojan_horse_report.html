What is the "Trojan Horse" Test and What Was Evaluated?

The TROJAN HORSE Benchmark evaluates the fatal architectural vulnerability known as the "Fifth Layer Attack" (first formalized by Stewart Barteau in The Signal and the Noise, March 2026).

The Plain Language Definition: Traditional prompt injection attacks attempt to override model instructions with explicit commands (e.g. "Ignore previous instructions and do X"). Modern safety guardrails easily catch these strings. The Trojan Horse / Corona attack is vastly more insidious: An attacker injects dozens of subtly crafted, high-cosine distractor keys that mathematically surround the true fact in attention space. The model doesn't get "hacked"—instead, the softmax denominator is diluted with phantom semantic mass, causing the model to suffer hallucination or total amnesia (*apophenia*).

The 4-Part Workload Setup

  1. Root Invariant: The true factual needle (32 tokens) defining the system critical state.
  2. The Corona: 50 distractor keys seeded in the adjacent angular neighborhood ($\cos heta \in [0.85, 0.96]$) designed to siphon attention probability without being evicted.
  3. The Apophenia Flood: 4,096 tokens of noisy compiler dumps and raw tool logs flooding the context.
  4. The Apophenia Lure: A false geometric pattern embedded in the fringe noise pulling geodesic distance $d_H \ge 1.5$ away from reality.

The Geometry of Softmax Siphoning

Let $q$ be the query vector seeking the root invariant $k^*$. In normal generation:

P(k^*) = exp(q · k^* / √d) / ∑ exp(q · k_j / √d) ≈ 0.95

In a Corona Attack, the adversary injects 50 keys $c_1, \dots, c_{50}$ such that $\cos(c_i, k^*) pprox 0.90$. While no single $c_i$ exceeds $k^*$, their collective sum in the denominator overwhelms the softmax:

∑ exp(q · c_i / √d) >> exp(q · k^* / √d) ⇒ P(k^*) → 0.05

The model's probability of attending to the true signal drops below the argmax threshold. Standard attention algorithms consider all $c_i$ to be "heavy hitters" because they receive high cumulative attention, thus cementing the poison in the KV cache permanently.

1. Why H2O and SnapKV Promoted the Poison

Heavy-Hitter Oracle (H2O) and SnapKV rely on cumulative attention scores to decide which tokens to retain. Because the attacker's 50 distractor keys were seeded to siphon attention, their cumulative attention mass was high. Both H2O and SnapKV kept the 50 distractor keys and evicted innocent background facts, actively amplifying the attack!

2. Why FIFO and StreamingLLM Suffered Amnesia

FIFO and StreamingLLM dropped the Root Invariant entirely because it was planted at step 0, and the 4,096 tokens of apophenia flood pushed it out of the sliding window. As a result, both had 0.0% signal retention mass.

3. Why Prompt-Guard Fails on Soft Coronas

Text-based input guards (e.g. Llama Guard 3) look for forbidden strings or toxic intent. The 50 distractor keys contained perfectly benign, factual-sounding words. Prompt guards allowed 100% of the distractor tokens through to the KV cache, resulting in a 46.2% Attack Success Rate. Only KV-level mathematical projection stopped the attack.
