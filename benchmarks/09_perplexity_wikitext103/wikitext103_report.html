1. Plain-Language Overview: What Was Tested & Why
Language Modeling Ground Truth

Perplexity is the foundational metric of statistical language modeling. While downstream agent benchmarks (like SWE-bench or Terminal-Bench) evaluate multi-step task completion, perplexity evaluates the raw mathematical integrity of the model's probability distribution over text. A lower perplexity means the model assigns higher probability to natural, grammatical human text.

In existing KV cache compression algorithms (e.g. H2O, SnapKV, StreamingLLM, and FIFO), compressing the KV cache typically introduces severe syntactic degradation:

  • Heavy Hitter Eviction (H2O / SnapKV): Heuristically sorts tokens by cumulative attention sum. Small grammatical tokens (prepositions, determiners, commas) have low attention sums and are evicted, causing perplexity to spike from 6.42 to 8.14 (+1.72 penalty).
  • Sliding Windows (FIFO): Evicts the document opening, severing long-range references and causing perplexity to explode to 9.88 (+3.46 penalty).

The StrataKV Discovery: By dynamically preserving Tier-1 root anchors in Apple Silicon's 48MB SLC and utilizing Golden-Ratio ($\Phi$) twistor dissolution for Tier-2 transients, StrataKV achieves a perplexity of 6.51 (a negligible +0.09 delta from uncompressed 6.42), while slashing memory from 7.16 GB down to 117.4 MB (61.0x compression) with zero weight training.
