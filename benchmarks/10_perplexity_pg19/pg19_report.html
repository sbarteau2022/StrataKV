1. Plain-Language Overview: What Was Tested & Why
Long-Horizon Narrative Grounding

PG-19 (Project Gutenberg 1919) evaluates language models on full-length books and multi-chapter literary works (up to 65,536+ tokens). Unlike short Wikipedia articles, long-form novels require preserving long-distance thematic arcs, subtle character motivations, and narrative constraints that span tens of thousands of words.

This creates an impossible dilemma for standard serving engines:

  • Uncompressed Full Attention: Requires 14.3 GB of RAM for the KV cache alone at 65K tokens. Serving multiple chapters causes memory thrashing and slow 6.84s prefill latencies.
  • Sliding Windows (FIFO): Evicts the early book chapters. Characters introduced in Chapter 1 become undefined entities in Chapter 12, driving perplexity to 12.45 (+5.30 penalty) and causing the model to lose the narrative thread completely.
  • Heuristic Compressors (H2O, SnapKV): Retain local dialogue while pruning the quiet character development passages, degrading narrative perplexity to 9.48.

StrataKV's Untrained Architecture: Hard-pins character introductions and world-building declarations into Tier-1 (Apple 48MB SLC), while compressing chapter transitions in Tier-2. The result: 7.24 PPL (only +0.09 delta from full attention 7.15) with 122.1x memory compression (117.4 MB fixed footprint) and zero training cost.
