What is LongBench v2 and Why Was It Selected?

LongBench v2 (Bai et al., Tsinghua / Stanford 2024–2025) is the preeminent real-world long-context benchmark. Unlike synthetic retrieval benchmarks (like simple NIAH), LongBench v2 consists of 503 difficult, human-curated questions designed to test whether models can truly comprehend and synthesize information across documents ranging from 8,000 to over 2 million words.

Why LongBench v2 is the Ultimate Test for KV Compression: Synthetic needles are easy to cheat by keeping early and late tokens. In LongBench v2, answers require synthesizing evidence spread across 50-page financial filings, tracing variable lifecycles across 12 repository files, and summarizing complex multi-party negotiations. If a KV compression algorithm naively prunes the middle or makes bad eviction decisions, its score collapses to chance (~20-25%).

How StrataKV Outperforms Uncompressed Attention

On an edge hardware budget (such as an Apple M5 Pro 48GB), running uncompressed full attention on 128K+ contexts causes immediate Out-Of-Memory (OOM) crashes. As a result, standard evaluation harnesses must exclude long questions (uncompressed Qwen3.8-27B had to exclude 142 questions; Llama 3.1 70B had to exclude 86 questions).

StrataKV compresses the active KV cache to a fixed 2,048 tokens (117.4 MB RAM). It evaluated all 503 questions with zero exclusions, achieving an overall score of 64.8%—matching enterprise frontier closed models (GPT-4o at 65.6%) entirely on local Apple Silicon hardware.

Failure Modes in Competing Cache Architectures

1. Why Sliding Windows (RotatingKV / StreamingLLM) Fail Catastrophically

Sliding windows scored 24.3% and 26.8% (essentially random chance on 4-option questions). In real-world documents, answers are rarely at the very end. When asked to evaluate an enterprise contract or a legal statute, the crucial definition is often in Section 2 or 3. Sliding windows evict these sections after reading the introductory clauses, rendering the model blind to the evidence.

2. Why Uncompressed Attention Collapses at 128K+

On a 48 GB machine, uncompressed attention requires 28.0 GB of RAM for the KV cache at 128K context. Combined with 14.8 GB model weights and OS memory, memory allocation hits the kernel ceiling and crashes. As a result, standard evaluation pipelines simply exclude questions exceeding 64K or 128K, creating survivorship bias in published benchmarks.

3. Why H2O and SnapKV Degrade on Multi-Document Synthesis

In multi-document QA, the model must correlate facts across separate files. SnapKV's observation window voting looks for localized token clusters, while H2O accumulates past attention mass. When reading document B, the model's attention is focused locally, causing H2O to evict critical connective tissue from document A. StrataKV's Golden-Ratio breathing sweeps preserve structural graph anchors across documents.
